### Abstract

**Introduction**  
Paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) are an imaging biomarker associated with more severe disability and pronounced injury within a lesion in multiple sclerosis (MS). PRLs were shown to lead to diffuse periplaque white matter damage, but the relationship between focal PRL development and the characteristics of general normal-appearing tissues in the brain and how it changes over time remains unclear.

**Objectives/Aims**  
Leveraging ultra-high field, 7-Tesla (7T) magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to investigate whether PRL development is associated with changes in normal-appearing brain tissues of MS patients longitudinally.

**Methods**  
17 MS patients who had new contrast-enhancing lesions (CELs) detected on routine clinical MRI were included in this study. MP2RAGE images, which include a T1 map that estimates T1 relaxation time (RT), were acquired at 7T within 4 weeks of CEL detection (baseline) and 6 and 12 months after. Lesions were manually segmented at each time point and subsequently classified either as PRL or non-PRL based on a 6-month 7T multi-echo gradient echo-phase image, followed by automated segmentation of normal-appearing white matter (NAWM), normal-appearing gray matter (NAGM), cerebral spinal fluid (CSF), and thalamus. Relationships between T1-RT within normal-appearing tissues and PRL status were evaluated using linear regression and generalized linear mixed effects models.

**Results**  
Patients with new CELs that developed into PRLs had higher average T1-RT in NAWM (p<0.01 and p<0.05, respectively) and NAGM (both p<0.05) at baseline and 12 months, compared with patients with no CELs developed into PRLs. Linear regression supported this relationship in NAWM at both time points (both p<0.05) and at baseline for NAGM (p<0.05). Volumes of NAWM and NAGM did not differ between patients who did or did not develop new PRLs, and T1-RT did not change significantly over 12 months for either group. In both right and left thalamus for both groups, T1-RT increased at 12 months compared with baseline (both p<0.05) but did not associate with new PRL development status.

**Conclusion**  
Longer T1-RT in NAWM and NAGM at baseline and 12 months is associated with new PRL development. This finding could be due to more widespread, chronic inflammation or degeneration in these normal-appearing tissues. Thalamic T1-RT increased over 12 months irrespective of new PRL development, potentially reflecting more global degenerative processes. Further investigation into the mechanisms of these changes with larger cohorts is needed.
