### Abstract

Histopathological review of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) slides is foundational for both biomedical discovery and clinical decision-making, yet it remains a major bottleneck in the spatial-omics era. Spatial omics assays are limited in capture area and can cost up to \$10,000 per slide, so successful experimental design demands careful tissue characterization and sample selection using histopathology. In parallel, clinical workflows rely on manual analysis to define disease phenotypes and guide treatment plans, but exhaustive manual review and precise boundary annotation are time-intensive and require years of training. These gaps in research and clinical settings delay treatment decisions and limit large-scale investigations.

We present ATHENA (Augmented Tissue Histopathology Evaluation and Navigation Assistant), an integrated computational pipeline that assists pathologists and researchers across key tissue analysis tasks. First, ATHENA performs high-efficiency, cellular-resolution segmentation of gigapixel whole-slide images (WSIs), enabling scalable investigations of tissue architecture across large cohorts with optimized compute and memory overhead. Second, ATHENA’s annotation module provides label propagation and refinement by learning from coarse or weak annotations. It further prioritizes “review hotspots” that are uncertain or sample-specific to direct expert attention onto where it is most informative. Third, ATHENA includes an ROI optimization module that recommends regions whose tissue composition is maximally representative of the WSI, improving objectivity and reproducibility in ROI selection and reducing the risk of missing molecular heterogeneity under constrained spatial-omics budgets. By unifying scalable segmentation, human-in-the-loop label refinement, and ROI optimization, ATHENA turns routine H&E into an actionable decision layer for experimental design and clinical triage.
